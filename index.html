PULL-UP PROGRAM

Going from 1–2 pull-ups to 10+ in 12 weeks is very achievable. The strategy: build strength with assisted variations, use negatives to build raw pulling power, and practice frequency without overtraining.

The Core Principle
3 Movements That Build Pull-Ups

① Band-Assisted Pull-Ups — builds the pattern and volume safely

② Negative Pull-Ups — builds raw eccentric strength the fastest

③ Lat Pulldown — strengthens the same muscles with controllable load

WEEKS 1–2 · Foundation
Goal: Learn the movement, build grip & lat awareness
Dead Hangs: 3 sets × 20–30 sec (every gym session)
Band-Assisted Pull-Ups: 3 × 5 reps (use a thick resistance band)
Lat Pulldown: 3 × 12 reps (moderate weight, full range)
Scapular Pull-Ups: 3 × 10 (hang, depress shoulder blades without bending elbows)
WEEKS 3–4 · Negatives Begin
Goal: Start negative pull-ups, reduce band assistance
Dead Hangs: 3 × 30 sec
Band-Assisted Pull-Ups: 4 × 6 reps (use a thinner band than week 1)
Negative Pull-Ups: 3 × 4 reps (5–6 sec descent, jump to get to top)
Lat Pulldown: 3 × 10 (add weight compared to week 1–2)
WEEKS 5–6 · First Unassisted Reps
Goal: Achieve 3–5 unassisted pull-ups
Unassisted Pull-Ups: Max effort × 3 sets (even if it's only 2–3 reps, that's progress)
Band-Assisted Pull-Ups: 3 × 8 (light band, for volume)
Negative Pull-Ups: 3 × 5 reps (aim for 6–8 sec descent)
Add weight to lat pulldown by 5–10 lbs
WEEKS 7–9 · Volume Build
Goal: Reach 6–8 unassisted pull-ups
Greasing the Groove: 3–4 sets of pull-ups spread throughout the day (never go to failure — stop 2 reps short)
Gym: 4 sets of pull-ups, max clean reps
Negative Pull-Ups: 3 × 6 (very slow descent, 8–10 sec)
Consider getting a doorframe pull-up bar for home practice
WEEKS 10–12 · 10+ Pull-Ups
Goal: Hit 10 clean pull-ups in a row
Pull-Up Clusters: 10 sets of 1–2 reps with 30s rest (accumulates volume without fatigue)
Test max rep sets every 2 weeks — track your progress
Add a 5–10 lb weight belt for 3 × 3 weighted pull-ups (makes bodyweight feel easier)
Keep negatives in rotation for continued strength gains
⚡ Grip Matters
Use a shoulder-width overhand grip (pronated). Don't let your elbows flare forward at the top. Think "elbows to hips" as you pull. This maximizes lat engagement and protects your rotator cuff.

Supplements Worth Considering
🧪 Simple Stack

Creatine Monohydrate — 5g daily. The most research-backed supplement for strength & muscle. Take anytime, consistency matters.

Whey Protein — Only if you're struggling to hit 190g protein from food. Supplement, don't replace.

Vitamin D3 + K2 — Especially important with a sedentary desk job (limited sun). Supports testosterone and bone health.

Magnesium Glycinate — 300–400mg before bed. Improves sleep quality and muscle recovery.

PRO TIPS

The difference between good results and great results is often these overlooked details.

😴 Sleep Is Your Anabolic Window
At 48, sleep quality directly impacts testosterone and recovery. Aim for 7–8 hours. Poor sleep is the #1 reason men over 40 plateau in the gym. Keep a consistent bedtime — even on weekends. Your magnesium and no-screen-before-bed habits will pay more dividends than any supplement.
🪑 Beat the Desk Job
Sitting 7–8 hours compresses your hip flexors and weakens your glutes, which directly limits squat depth and lower back health. Do this: stand or walk for 5 minutes every hour. Set a timer. Do 10 bodyweight squats or hip circles at your desk. This will dramatically improve your leg day performance and reduce lower-back tightness.
📈 Progressive Overload is Everything
If you lift the same weights every week, nothing changes. Track your lifts in a notes app. Each week, try to add 2.5–5 lbs OR do one more rep. Small consistent increases compound dramatically over months. This single habit separates people who transform their bodies from those who plateau.
💧 Hydration at Your Desk
Keep a 32 oz water bottle at your desk and finish two of them by the end of the work day. Dehydration kills strength and muscle-protein synthesis. Add electrolytes (a pinch of salt + a squeeze of lemon) if you sweat heavily during workouts.
🥒 Pickleball is an Underrated Asset
Don't underestimate weekend pickleball. It burns 400–600 calories/hour, trains rotational core strength, and keeps you active without feeling like exercise. The cable woodchop in your Friday workout directly transfers to pickleball power. Keep it — it's a long-term lifestyle habit that protects your heart health.
🧬 Recovery at 48 vs 28
Your muscles still respond very well to training, but recovery takes a day longer than it did in your 20s. This is why the 4-day program with built-in rest is ideal. Don't be tempted to add a 5th heavy day. The Wednesday rest day and Sunday dog walk are part of the program — not gaps in it.
📅 The 12-Week Check-In
After 12 weeks, reassess: how many pull-ups can you do? How do your pants fit? Take a photo on day 1 and day 84 — the mirror lies, photos don't. If you've been consistent with training and nutrition, you should see 6–10 lbs of fat loss, noticeable shoulder and arm definition, and meaningful leg strength gains. Adjust the plan from there.
One More Thing
🔑 Consistency Over Perfection

Missing one workout doesn't matter. Missing 3 in a row starts to. Life happens — travel, illness, stress. The rule: never miss twice in a row. A mediocre workout done is infinitely better than a perfect workout skipped. You already have the discipline — you go to the gym 4 times a week and take the dogs out on weekends. That foundation is everything. Now it's just about directing it smarter.
